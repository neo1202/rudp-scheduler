# Benchmarks on a kernel-impaired network (netem)

Generated by `netem/run.sh`. Do not edit by hand; rerun instead.

Unlike [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md), nothing here is simulated by this
repository: 1 server, 4 workers and 1 client run in separate containers on a
Docker bridge network, and each container impairs its own outgoing packets with
the Linux kernel's `tc netem` (the delay and loss rate shown, applied
independently to each direction). The scheduler, the workers and the job
are identical in every row; only `-transport` changes.

- Job: 40 000 000 nonces in 400 chunks of 100 000; 10 measured jobs per row
  after one warm-up job, one connection per job, every answer checked.
- Host: 12 CPUs, Docker Desktop, kernel 6.10.14-linuxkit, aarch64.
- TCP is the kernel's (CUBIC, with congestion control); the reliable-UDP
  transport has a fixed window of 5 and no congestion control. Read the
  comparison as "what does this workload lose to in-order delivery and to
  loss-as-congestion", not as "this transport is better than TCP".

## Reading the numbers

- **No loss: no difference.** The custom transport costs nothing when the
  network behaves. The job is CPU bound and both transports keep the workers fed.
- **Loss hurts TCP's tail first.** At 1 ms and 5% loss the medians are 595 ms
  vs 670 ms, but the slowest job is 605 ms vs 1972 ms: one connection that hits
  a retransmission timeout stalls every result queued behind the lost segment,
  and that worker's window stays full. Delivery on arrival confines a loss to
  the message that was lost.
- **At 10% loss the gap is about 1.8x** at both delays (635 vs 1132 ms, 1923 vs
  3225 ms).
- **The kernel agrees with the simulator.** The reliable-UDP rows at 1 ms
  (533 / 595 / 635 ms at 0 / 5 / 10% loss) match what `internal/lossy` produced
  in [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md) (519 / 579 / 619 ms), measured by a
  different mechanism in a different environment.
- **What this does not show.** At 20 ms delay both transports take twice as
  long even without loss. That is the scheduler, not the transport: with at
  most 5 chunks in flight per worker and a 40 ms round trip, a worker computes
  its 5 chunks (about 25 ms) and then waits. On a high-latency link `-window`
  and the chunk size need to grow. And TCP backs off because it treats loss as
  congestion, which is the right thing to do on a shared link; this transport
  has a fixed window and does not. It wins here partly by not being polite.
